## Per-figure decisions

### Figure 1.1 — Symmetry in Nature and Physics — PASS
**Science/editorial: PASS. Style: PASS.** The full-frame title is correctly mapped to the opening figure. Biological forms are recognisably approximate, while the lattice and snowflake represent idealised crystalline order; field lines are a plausible dipole-style schematic. This is consistent with manuscript Figure 1.1’s broad caption and its cautious suggestion that symmetry may be a deep organising principle. Keep that distinction clear in accessible copy: bilateral resemblance alone is not proof of mathematical invariance. **No image repair required.**

### Figure 2.1 — Natural Symmetry Patterns — FIX
**Science/editorial: FIX. Style: PASS.** The sunflower is overlaid with a golden rectangle/Fibonacci-style spiral. Without an “illustrative approximation” cue, it reads as a fitted exact law for this specimen. The article discusses diverse spirals arising for different biological and physical reasons; its Figure 2.1 caption does not assert a golden-ratio fit. **Recommended correction:** remove the exact-fit overlay in a regenerated image, or clearly mark it as schematic/approximate and not a measurement or universal rule. Do not use the present image unchanged to support an exact golden-ratio claim.

### Figure 3.1 — Mathematical Symmetry and Invariance — FIX
**Science/editorial: FIX. Style: PASS.** The 60° rotation and mirror panels can represent the ideal regular sixfold shape; the `D₆ = ⟨R₆₀°, Mₓ⟩` box includes rotations/reflections of a hexagon and does not include translation. The translation panel correctly looks like a transformation example rather than an equation claiming the snowflake is unchanged. However, its placement under the overall invariance graphic risks conflation. `T₍ₐ,ᵦ₎S(x,y)=S(x+a,y+b)` leaves the active/passive convention unstated; `Mₓ` is ambiguous for the depicted x-coordinate reversal; duplicated x/y axis glyphs weaken legibility. A finite snowflake is not translation-invariant, though translation preserves its congruence, area and perimeter. **Recommended correction:** label this a generic translation transformation; clarify the convention and reflection axis; identify the snowflake as idealised; remove duplicate axes; state that a finite object changes position rather than remaining in place.

### Figure 4.1 — Noether’s Theorem and Conservation Laws — PASS
**Science/editorial: PASS. Style: PASS.** The figure presents one correct correspondence: continuous rotational invariance → Noether’s theorem → conserved angular momentum (`L = const.`). It is a conceptual schematic, not a derivation. The manuscript caption correctly states the rotation/angular-momentum mapping. **Caption requirement for any later site use:** state that the symmetry is a continuous rotational symmetry of the relevant action/system; for the mechanical picture, make clear that conservation assumes no external torque. This is an explanatory qualification, not an image defect.

### Figure 5.1 — Broken Symmetry and Emergence — PASS
**Science/editorial: PASS. Style: PASS.** The lower plot is a labelled one-dimensional double-well `V(φ)` with two degenerate minima, not a full circular Mexican-hat potential. The upper transition from a more symmetric state to domains is clearly conceptual and consistent with the manuscript’s symmetry-breaking section. The artwork does not identify a universal Higgs mechanism or claim all masses arise identically. **Caption recommendation:** explicitly call the curve a schematic one-dimensional double-well, not a full Mexican-hat field surface or universal mass-generation model.

### Figure 6.1 — The SYMMETRIA Symmetry Perspective — FIX
**Science/editorial: FIX. Style: FIX.** The manuscript’s visible caption is appropriately tentative (“explores whether”), but the artwork itself has no “proposal/untested” qualifier. “The SYMMETRIA” is the dominant, masthead-like heading, contrary to the locked no-project-masthead rule even though it repeats the section name. The composition assembles natural forms, a Newton’s cradle, a planetary-orbit atom, a potential well and abstract geometry rather than explaining one specific relation. The atom resembles the obsolete miniature-solar-system model. **Recommended correction:** regenerate as one focused cinematic visualization of an explicitly exploratory symmetry hypothesis; use a non-masthead subject title; remove the planetary atom and unrelated gallery; place a clear “hypothesis under investigation” qualifier in the artwork or adjacent accessible copy.
