## BridgePoint — 16 lessons

**Audience:** curious adults and newcomers who can build mathematical understanding progressively. Each lesson should contain a plain-English explanation, prerequisites, carefully explained notation, at least one fully worked example, a recap and an optional *Going Deeper* route.

### Category 1 — The Mathematical Toolkit

| No. | Lesson title | Learning outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 01 | **The Language of Mathematics** | Interpret variables, functions, powers, equations and scientific notation |
| 02 | **Vectors, Geometry and Space** | Understand direction, magnitude, coordinates and simple spatial transformations |
| 03 | **Calculus: Change and Motion** | Understand derivatives/integrals through velocity, acceleration and accumulated quantities |
| 04 | **Complex Numbers and Waves** | Use real and imaginary components to describe phase and oscillation |

### Category 2 — Symmetry and Mathematical Structure

| No. | Lesson title | Learning outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 05 | **Matrices and Transformations** | Follow simple matrix operations and visualise rotations or other transformations |
| 06 | **Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors** | Explain invariant directions and why eigenproblems appear in physical models |
| 07 | **Symmetry, Groups and Invariance** | Recognise transformations that leave mathematical/physical relationships unchanged |
| 08 | **Differential Equations and Conservation** | Connect rates of change to model equations and introduce conservation principles |

### Category 3 — Quantum Theory and Nilpotent Algebra

| No. | Lesson title | Learning outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 09 | **Quantum States and Probability** | Distinguish quantum amplitudes, measurement probabilities and interpretation |
| 10 | **Operators, Commutators and Observables** | Explain operators through simple examples and learn why order can matter |
| 11 | **Spin, Spinors and the Dirac Equation** | Meet spinor notation and the role of Dirac's relativistic description |
| 12 | **Clifford Algebra and Nilpotency** | Work through simple zero-square examples and distinguish established mathematics from extensions |

### Category 4 — Mathematics for Space and Research

| No. | Lesson title | Learning outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 13 | **Spacetime and Special Relativity** | Understand events, reference frames and basic spacetime intervals |
| 14 | **Gravity, Curvature and General Relativity** | Introduce curved spacetime conceptually while identifying mathematical prerequisites |
| 15 | **Orbits, Propulsion and Energy** | Apply basic mechanics and conservation to space trajectories and propulsion constraints |
| 16 | **From Equations to Research Questions** | Learn to distinguish a hypothesis, derivation, simulation and test; see how NSRP and SEIP fit together |

The space-facing final category motivates earlier mathematical learning without pretending all preceding topics are easy. Advanced equations should be expandable and optional where appropriate.


## NSRP — five research projects, four phases in each

**Nilpotent Symmetry Research Programme.** Present each of the **five stage projects** on the NSRP website page, with its **four phases** as subordinate targets. Distinguish proposed research from results. Where source materials are only folder plans or outline documents, mark status as *planned* and do not imply the phases are completed.

### Project 1 — NCP: Nilpotent Consistency Project

The NCP is validation-first: determine what the nilpotent formulation is mathematically and what it does or does not support, including comparison with Dirac and Clifford algebra. The four high-level phase labels below follow the historical companion SEIP/EFP crosswalk; detailed NCP phase material must be reconciled before publication of expanded descriptions.

| Phase | Research scope |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 — Foundations | Formal definitions, objects, notation, underlying nilpotent assumptions |
| 2 — Structure | Internal algebraic properties, closure, identities and equivalence checks |
| 3 — Extensions | Investigate any mathematically defensible extensions, including failures |
| 4 — Interpretations | Assess relationships to established physics without overstating results |

### Project 2 — SEP: Symmetry Emergence Project

| Phase | Historical phase focus |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 — Algebraic Extension | Formal proposed extensions of the baseline structure |
| 2 — Resonance Classification | Classify defined resonant/structural mathematical models |
| 3 — Functional Mapping | Examine formal correspondences and functions |
| 4 — Initial SM/GR Correspondence | Evaluate possible relationships to Standard Model / General Relativity descriptions |

### Project 3 — SDEP: SYMMETRIA Deepening Project

| Phase | Historical phase focus |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 — Standard Model Emergence | Examine mathematically precise proposed particle/interaction correspondences |
| 2 — General Relativity Emergence | Explore possible gravitational/spacetime relationships |
| 3 — Observer Boundary Formalism | Define observer/boundary questions without conflating interpretation with evidence |
| 4 — Unified Closure | Evaluate compatibility, completeness and unresolved inconsistencies |

### Project 4 — SVP: SYMMETRIA Validation Project

| Phase | Historical phase focus |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 — Nilpotent System Simulation | Specify explicit reproducible mathematical/computational tests |
| 2 — Mode Interaction Modelling | Examine interactions and quantitative behaviour in bounded models |
| 3 — Emergent Structure Classification | Classify observed *model* results, not claimed physical phenomena |
| 4 — Physical Correspondence Validation | Compare with accepted theory/observations, uncertainties and possible falsification |

### Project 5 — SMP: SYMMETRIA Maturation Project

| Phase | Historical phase focus |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 — Consistency and Closure Analysis | Review unresolved mathematics, contradictions and reproducibility |
| 2 — Boundary Formalism Refinement | Revise and constrain proposed boundary descriptions |
| 3 — Cross-Domain Validation | Evaluate whether any claims withstand independent cross-domain checks |
| 4 — Final Framework Statement | Produce a qualified synthesis listing evidence, limits and open problems |

**NSRP count: 5 projects × 4 phases = 20.** Research should progress only when supported by evidence; do not portray the five projects as a guaranteed sequence of successful scientific breakthroughs.
