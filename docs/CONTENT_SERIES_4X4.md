# Four-by-four learning and research programme catalogue

**Date:** 16 November 2026  
**Version:** 1.0 proposal  
**Status:** All entries **planned**. No BridgePoint lessons or NSRP/SEIP targets in this catalogue are asserted to be written, achieved, validated or published.

## Purpose

Like the existing [SYMMETRIA Discussion Series](../pages/discussion-series.html), each proposed programme has **four categories of four**. Their outputs are deliberately different:

- **BridgePoint:** 16 approachable *lessons* to equip readers without a theoretical physics background.
- **NSRP:** 16 *scientific research targets* for rigorous mathematical and physical assessment.
- **SEIP:** 16 *computational/engineering research targets* focused especially on space exploration and feasibility.

Each programme should display **planned/in progress/completed with evidence** clearly. The numbering is a readable learning/work plan, not proof that earlier topics are finished or that speculative physics has been validated.

## BridgePoint — 16 lessons

**Audience:** curious adults and newcomers who can build mathematical understanding progressively. Each lesson should contain a plain-English explanation, prerequisites, carefully explained notation, at least one fully worked example, a recap and an optional *Going Deeper* route.

### Category 1 — The Mathematical Toolkit

| No. | Lesson title | Learning outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 01 | **The Language of Mathematics** | Interpret variables, functions, powers, equations and scientific notation |
| 02 | **Vectors, Geometry and Space** | Understand direction, magnitude, coordinates and simple spatial transformations |
| 03 | **Calculus: Change and Motion** | Understand derivatives/integrals through velocity, acceleration and accumulated quantities |
| 04 | **Complex Numbers and Waves** | Use real and imaginary components to describe phase and oscillation |

### Category 2 — Symmetry and Mathematical Structure

| No. | Lesson title | Learning outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 05 | **Matrices and Transformations** | Follow simple matrix operations and visualise rotations or other transformations |
| 06 | **Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors** | Explain invariant directions and why eigenproblems appear in physical models |
| 07 | **Symmetry, Groups and Invariance** | Recognise transformations that leave mathematical/physical relationships unchanged |
| 08 | **Differential Equations and Conservation** | Connect rates of change to model equations and introduce conservation principles |

### Category 3 — Quantum Theory and Nilpotent Algebra

| No. | Lesson title | Learning outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 09 | **Quantum States and Probability** | Distinguish quantum amplitudes, measurement probabilities and interpretation |
| 10 | **Operators, Commutators and Observables** | Explain operators through simple examples and learn why order can matter |
| 11 | **Spin, Spinors and the Dirac Equation** | Meet spinor notation and the role of Dirac's relativistic description |
| 12 | **Clifford Algebra and Nilpotency** | Work through simple zero-square examples and distinguish established mathematics from extensions |

### Category 4 — Mathematics for Space and Research

| No. | Lesson title | Learning outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 13 | **Spacetime and Special Relativity** | Understand events, reference frames and basic spacetime intervals |
| 14 | **Gravity, Curvature and General Relativity** | Introduce curved spacetime conceptually while identifying mathematical prerequisites |
| 15 | **Orbits, Propulsion and Energy** | Apply basic mechanics and conservation to space trajectories and propulsion constraints |
| 16 | **From Equations to Research Questions** | Learn to distinguish a hypothesis, derivation, simulation and test; see how NSRP and SEIP fit together |

The space-facing final category motivates earlier mathematical learning without pretending all preceding topics are easy. Advanced equations should be expandable and optional where appropriate.

## NSRP — 16 proposed scientific research targets

**Nilpotent Symmetry Research Programme.** These are **research questions/targets**, not promised scientific findings. Each target requires formal definitions, source references, assumptions, comparisons with accepted mathematics/physics, possible refutations and a record of limitations.

### Category 1 — Mathematical Foundations and Consistency

| No. | Research target | Acceptance/critical test |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 01 | **Define the Nilpotent Framework Precisely** | Record generators, operations, notation and assumptions without ambiguity |
| 02 | **Test Closure, Identities and Nilpotency** | Supply reproducible algebraic derivations and edge-case/counterexample checks |
| 03 | **Compare with Dirac and Clifford Structures** | Determine what is equivalent, different or not yet established |
| 04 | **Document Equivalence, Failures and Open Problems** | Publish an internal evidence register including negative and unresolved results |

### Category 2 — Symmetry and Possible Extensions

| No. | Research target | Acceptance/critical test |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 05 | **Formalise Symmetries and Transformations** | Specify symmetries in mathematically checkable terms |
| 06 | **Examine Proposed Algebraic Extensions** | Prove basic properties or identify where constructions fail |
| 07 | **Check Constraints and Conservation Relations** | Compare proposed results with established conservation requirements |
| 08 | **Separate Emergence Models from Physical Claims** | State what toy structures do and do not imply about the physical world |

### Category 3 — Validation and Predictive Discipline

| No. | Research target | Acceptance/critical test |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 09 | **Identify Distinguishing Mathematical Consequences** | Find results not guaranteed by baseline theories, if any exist |
| 10 | **Compare Against Established Physical Models** | Test consistency with relevant quantum, relativistic and field descriptions |
| 11 | **Construct Reproducible Computational Checks** | Record algorithms, test cases, assumptions and numerical limits |
| 12 | **Specify Falsification, Uncertainty and Review Criteria** | Define what would refute or weaken each proposed claim |

### Category 4 — Implications for Space Exploration

| No. | Research target | Acceptance/critical test |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 13 | **Assess Spacetime and Gravity Questions** | Identify legitimate, testable implications without assuming new gravity effects |
| 14 | **Examine Vacuum and Field-Energy Constraints** | Check dimensional consistency, energy accounting and established limits |
| 15 | **Look for Space-Relevant Observational Tests** | Assess whether any claim suggests an observable discriminator in astrophysics or space science |
| 16 | **Synthesis, Independent Critique and Next Questions** | Distinguish supported findings from conjecture and decide which questions merit further investigation |

Historical NCP/SEP/SDEP/SVP/SMP names can be cross-referenced within this simplified sequence, but need not be recreated as five numbered project folders or claimed as completed stages.

## SEIP — 16 proposed computational and engineering targets

**SymTech Engineering & Innovation Programme.** Each target should offer a realistic standard-PC route where feasible, generally Python-based, before considering advanced facilities. Baseline models, conservation laws, uncertainty and honest failure reporting are mandatory. This is a **research/engineering programme**, not a claim of functioning spacecraft hardware.

### Category 1 — Reliable Computational Foundations

| No. | Engineering target | Demonstration or exit criterion |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 01 | **Establish a Reproducible Python Toolchain** | Document environments, inputs, tests and rerun instructions |
| 02 | **Build Classical-Mechanics Reference Models** | Reproduce simple motion/orbit baselines with known analytic checks |
| 03 | **Track Units, Energy and Momentum** | Reject dimensionally inconsistent or conservation-violating models |
| 04 | **Test Numerical Error and Stability** | Compare methods, step sizes, error bounds and failure modes |

### Category 2 — Modelling Space Exploration Systems

| No. | Engineering target | Demonstration or exit criterion |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 05 | **Model Orbits and Trajectory Changes** | Compare idealised trajectories to standard orbital mechanics |
| 06 | **Explore Spacecraft Thermal and Structural Limits** | Build bounded load, temperature or material-constraint analyses |
| 07 | **Compare Established Propulsion Concepts** | Use quantitative thrust, specific impulse, energy and mass baselines |
| 08 | **Examine Navigation and Mission Constraints** | Model uncertainty, communications, timing and operational trade-offs |

### Category 3 — Frontier Concepts and Physical Feasibility

| No. | Engineering target | Demonstration or exit criterion |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 09 | **Build Carefully Labelled Theory-Inspired Toy Models** | Make clear when outputs are purely mathematical and not physical predictions |
| 10 | **Stress-Test Novel Energy or Propulsion Proposals** | Check conservation, physical bounds and reasons a concept may not work |
| 11 | **Compare Relativistic and Gravitational Reference Cases** | Validate calculations against established physics before exploring alternatives |
| 12 | **Identify Potential Measurements and Discriminators** | Specify what would have to be measured to distinguish a proposal from a baseline |

### Category 4 — Verification, Review and Communication

| No. | Engineering target | Demonstration or exit criterion |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 13 | **Reproduce Key Computational Baselines** | Repeat results independently or record inability to reproduce |
| 14 | **Assess Sensitivity, Safety and Failure Modes** | Document uncertainties, plausible errors and constraints on interpretation |
| 15 | **Prepare Evidence-Gated Feasibility Reports** | State what is established, simulated, untested or infeasible |
| 16 | **Compile a Review-Ready Research Record** | Make code, assumptions and findings available for separate review and any later approved release |

**No flight, medical, physical-experiment or procurement approval is implied by completing a simulation.**

## Publication model and relationship to SYMMETRIA

- **SYMMETRIA:** existing 16-part **Discussion Series**, retain separately, with each item clearly labelled for online-discussion status.
- **BridgePoint:** lessons become publicly readable *only as they are actually authored, checked and approved*.
- **NSRP:** present research targets and status; do not invent results or write them as if completed.
- **SEIP:** present engineering targets and status; do not claim technologies work.
- **Publications:** remains **empty** until an explicit owner decision places a qualifying formal research output there.
