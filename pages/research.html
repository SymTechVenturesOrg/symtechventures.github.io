Independent research / Space exploration

Research directions

The project's main interest is space exploration, approached through foundational physics and careful computational investigation. This is a long-term research direction, not a claim to possess new spacecraft technology or confirmed physical mechanisms.

Exploratory work: a mathematical proposal or computer model is not evidence of a new physical effect. This page describes questions and planned investigations, not completed results.

How the programmes connect

SYMMETRIA

Develops exploratory theoretical questions and proposed mathematical structures.

NSRP

Asks whether ideas are well-defined, mathematically consistent and distinguishable from existing descriptions.

SEIP

Explores bounded computational models and engineering constraints, returning limitations and questions to the theoretical work.

BridgePoint

Builds background knowledge to help readers understand and question the research.

SYMMETRIA, NSRP and SEIP inform one another: critical mathematical or engineering findings can reshape the questions and proposed structures.

Questions before conclusions

A research question is not yet a mathematical statement. A mathematical formulation can be compared with established results. A simulation explores a defined model; it is not an independent measurement of nature. Measurement, if feasible, requires suitable instruments, methods and scrutiny.

Question → Mathematical formulation → Critical comparison → Reproducible modelling → Independent measurement, if feasible. This is a research approach, not a declaration that each step has occurred.

Why space exploration?

Space exploration raises demanding questions about motion, gravity, time, energy, measurement and the physical limits of technology. Established physics provides useful contexts for evaluating speculative questions without confusing possibilities with demonstrated engineering.
